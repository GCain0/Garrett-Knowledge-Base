# Organizing System Analysis: Halo Fanon Wiki

---

## Overview

The **Halo Fanon Wiki** is a community-built space where Halo fans expand the universe with their own ideas  new Spartans, custom factions, original planets, and entire storylines that don’t exist in official canon. Because everything is fan-created, the wiki has to organize a huge mix of imaginative content while still feeling familiar to Halo fans. This analysis looks at how the wiki structures its pages, how people interact with it, and how all those fictional pieces connect to each other.

---

## Primary Resources Being Organized

The main resources on the Halo Fanon Wiki are **fan-made articles**, which cover a wide range of fictional elements:

- **Characters** — custom Spartans, alien leaders, rogue soldiers  
- **Factions and groups** — UNSC units, Covenant splinter groups, new alien species  
- **Weapons, vehicles, and tech** — modified rifles, experimental armor, new ship classes  
- **Planets and locations** — colonies, battlefields, home worlds  
- **Stories and timelines** — full campaigns, personal character arcs, historical events  

Each page blends creative writing with an encyclopedia-style layout, which helps keep the fanon universe readable even when dozens of people contribute.

---

## Primary Interactions Supported

People use the Halo Fanon Wiki in several ways:

- **Exploring fan-made lore:** Readers jump between pages to learn about characters, factions, or tech created by other fans.  
- **Worldbuilding:** Writers use the wiki to keep their own stories consistent and build off ideas from other contributors.  
- **Creating and editing:** Anyone can add new pages or update existing ones, which keeps the universe growing.  
- **Researching fan canon:** Even though it’s fictional, fans often check the wiki to see how certain characters or groups fit into the larger fan-made timeline.  
- **Community discussion:** Talk pages and comments let contributors coordinate, debate lore decisions, or ask questions.

The wiki acts as both a creative playground and a shared reference library.

---

## Classification System Used

The wiki uses a **hybrid classification system** that fits the flexible nature of fan-created content:

- **Hierarchical categories:** Big buckets like *Characters*, *Weapons*, *Planets*, and *Factions* organize the main types of content.  
- **Subcategories:** These break things down further — for example, *Human Weapons* vs. *Covenant Weapons*.  
- **Faceted classification:** Pages often sit in multiple categories at once. A custom Spartan might be in *Characters*, *UNSC*, and *Military Personnel*.  
- **Info box templates:** These give structure to pages by listing things like species, rank, affiliation, creator, and first appearance.

This setup works well because it mirrors the organization of official Halo wikis while still giving contributors plenty of freedom.

---

## Representation of Relationships Between Resources

The wiki shows relationships between pages through several features:

- **Hyperlinks:** Most pages link directly to related characters, factions, battles, or technologies.  
- **Categories:** These help readers find similar or connected content.  
- **Info box links:** Affiliations, species, and ranks often link to broader lore pages.  
- **Navigation templates:** Some pages include templates listing related units, ships, or story arcs.  
- **“See also” sections:** These point readers toward other relevant fanon entries.

Together, these tools create a web of interconnected fan-made lore that’s easy to explore, even for new readers.
